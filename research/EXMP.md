## Thesis

The durable question is whether EXMP can keep converting its position into cash
earnings over a holding period measured in years rather than weeks. The thesis
rests on the economics of the business, not on recent price action, and it is
written so that a later run can disagree with it on the evidence rather than
re-derive it from nothing.

## Bull case

Demand is contracted rather than speculative, the balance sheet carries no
refinancing risk across the stated horizon, and the competitive position rests
on switching costs that take years to erode. If that holds, the current
valuation understates the cash the business generates over the horizon.

## Bear case

The contracted visibility may be shorter than it appears, the margin assumption
behind any valuation here is estimated rather than disclosed, and a well-funded
competitor could compress pricing before the horizon ends. A good business at a
poor entry price is still a poor investment.

## Risks

Cyclical demand, customer concentration, regulatory change, and the plain
possibility that the thesis is wrong. For the crypto framework specifically,
drawdowns of 70-90% are treated as a plausible risk rather than a tail case, so
24 months is a short horizon for that asset class.

## What would change the classification

A sustained deterioration in the cash-conversion trend, a disclosed change to
the contracted order book, or a price move large enough that the entry no longer
carries a margin for error. Any of those sends this note back to the research
queue rather than straight to a purchase.
